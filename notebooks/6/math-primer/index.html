Determinant¶

For a square matrix A∈Rn×nA\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}A∈Rn×n, expansion along any row iii gives

det⁡(A)=∑j=1n(−1)i+jAijdet⁡(Aij),\det(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+j}A_{ij}\det(A_{ij}),det(A)=j=1∑n​(−1)i+jAij​det(Aij​),
(2)

where AijA_{ij}Aij​ is the submatrix formed by deleting row iii and column jjj. Useful properties are

det⁡(AB)=det⁡(A)det⁡(B),det⁡(AT)=det⁡(A),det⁡(αA)=αndet⁡(A),det⁡(I)=1.\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B),\qquad \det(A^\mathsf{T})=\det(A),\qquad \det(\alpha A)=\alpha^n\det(A),\qquad \det(I)=1.det(AB)=det(A)det(B),det(AT)=det(A),det(αA)=αndet(A),det(I)=1.
(3)

The exponent nnn in the scaling law matters: multiplying the whole matrix by α\alphaα scales each of its nnn rows. A square matrix is singular exactly when det⁡(A)=0\det(A)=0det(A)=0.

Rank¶

Invertible Matrix Theorem¶

For a square n×nn\times nn×n matrix AAA, the following statements are equivalent:

  • AAA is invertible; its row-reduced form is III; and it has nnn pivots.

  • Ax=0Ax=0Ax=0 has only the trivial solution, so Nul⁡(A)={0}\operatorname{Nul}(A)=\{0\}Nul(A)={0} and dim⁡Nul⁡(A)=0\dim\operatorname{Nul}(A)=0dimNul(A)=0.

  • The columns of AAA are linearly independent, form a basis of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, and span Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn; equivalently rank⁡(A)=n\operatorname{rank}(A)=nrank(A)=n.

  • The map x↦Axx\mapsto Axx↦Ax is both one-to-one and onto; therefore Ax=bAx=bAx=b has a solution for every b∈Rnb\in\mathbb{R}^nb∈Rn.

  • A left or right inverse exists; ATA^\mathsf{T}AT is invertible; det⁡(A)≠0\det(A)\ne0det(A)=0; and zero is not an eigenvalue.

  • The row space is Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, (Col⁡A)⊥={0}(\operatorname{Col}A)^\perp=\{0\}(ColA)⊥={0}, (Nul⁡A)⊥=Rn(\operatorname{Nul}A)^\perp=\mathbb{R}^n(NulA)⊥=Rn, and AAA has nnn nonzero singular values.

These equivalences consolidate Lay’s Invertible Matrix Theorem into one searchable list.

Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues¶

For square AAA, a nonzero vector vvv is an eigenvector with eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ when Av=λvAv=\lambda vAv=λv. Thus A−λIA-\lambda IA−λI is singular and eigenvalues are roots of det⁡(A−λI)=0\det(A-\lambda I)=0det(A−λI)=0. Counting algebraic multiplicity, det⁡(A)=∏iλi\det(A)=\prod_i\lambda_idet(A)=∏i​λi​.

If AAA is real and symmetric, the spectral theorem gives A=VΛVTA=V\Lambda V^\mathsf{T}A=VΛVT with real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors. Only under this symmetry assumption can definiteness be read directly from eigenvalue signs: all positive means positive definite, all nonnegative means positive semidefinite, and mixed signs mean indefinite.

A nonsymmetric real matrix may have complex eigenvalues, and its eigenvalues do not in general classify the quadratic form xTAxx^\mathsf{T}AxxTAx. That form is governed by the symmetric part (A+AT)/2(A+A^\mathsf{T})/2(A+AT)/2. For rectangular matrices, singular values σi≥0\sigma_i\ge0σi​≥0—the square roots of the eigenvalues of ATAA^\mathsf{T}AATA—play the analogous role.

Vector and Matrix Norms¶

A vector norm ∣∣x∣∣||x||∣∣x∣∣ measures the length of a vector. It maps Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn into the nonnegative reals and satisfies three properties:

∣∣x∣∣=0⇒x=0,∣∣αx∣∣=∣α∣⋅∣∣x∣∣,∣∣x+y∣∣≤∣∣x∣∣+∣∣y∣∣||x|| = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0, \qquad ||\alpha x|| = |\alpha| \cdot ||x||, \qquad ||x + y|| \leq ||x|| + ||y||∣∣x∣∣=0⇒x=0,∣∣αx∣∣=∣α∣⋅∣∣x∣∣,∣∣x+y∣∣≤∣∣x∣∣+∣∣y∣∣
(19)

The last one is the triangle inequality. The ppp-norm is ∣∣x∣∣p=(∑i=1n∣xi∣p)1/p||x||_p = \left( \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|^p \right)^{1/p}∣∣x∣∣p​=(∑i=1n​∣xi​∣p)1/p for p≥1p \geq 1p≥1, and three cases matter here:

  • ∣∣x∣∣1=∑i=1n∣xi∣||x||_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|∣∣x∣∣1​=∑i=1n​∣xi​∣ --- the 1-norm
