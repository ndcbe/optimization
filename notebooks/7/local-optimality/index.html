Unconstrained comparison¶

For twice continuously differentiable objectives, a local unconstrained minimum has zero gradient and positive-semidefinite Hessian. These are necessary conditions; they do not classify every stationary point. The definitions, derivative tests and proofs have their existing home in Unconstrained Optimality Conditions. Here the feasible neighborhood and constraint reactions change the test.

Balance of Forces Interpretation¶

Here is a helpful handout.

Consider min⁡f(x)\min f(\mathbf{x})minf(x) subject to g(x)≤0g(\mathbf{x})\le0g(x)≤0. The objective is a landscape; its downhill force is −∇f-\nabla f−∇f. The fence force is −u∇g-u\nabla g−u∇g, with u≥0u\ge0u≥0, pointing toward the feasible side.

The drawn arrows are gradients, pointing toward increasing values. At a regular constrained minimum with an active pushing fence,

∇f(x∗)+u∗∇g(x∗)=0.\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*)+u^*\nabla g(\mathbf{x}^*)=0.∇f(x∗)+u∗∇g(x∗)=0.
(1)

The objective contour is tangent to the fence. Opposite gradient directions do not require equal lengths: the multiplier supplies the scale. Rescaling a constraint changes the numerical multiplier, so raw arrow lengths are not force magnitudes.

Source: Biegler (2010), Figures 4.5–4.6, pp.68–69. The handout and website use this same prepared drawing.

An equality h(x)=0h(\mathbf{x})=0h(x)=0 acts as a rail. Its force −v∇h-v\nabla h−v∇h can point either way, because vvv is unrestricted.

Balance is ∇f+u∇g+v∇h=0\nabla f+u\nabla g+v\nabla h=0∇f+u∇g+v∇h=0. For this illustrative drawing the normalized gradients are approximately

∇f=(0.9825,0.1861)T,∇g=(−0.8587,−0.5125)T,∇h=(−0.8480,0.5300)T.\nabla f=(0.9825,0.1861)^T,\quad \nabla g=(-0.8587,-0.5125)^T,\quad \nabla h=(-0.8480,0.5300)^T.∇f=(0.9825,0.1861)T,∇g=(−0.8587,−0.5125)T,∇h=(−0.8480,0.5300)T.
(2)

They give u≈0.76265>0u\approx0.76265>0u≈0.76265>0, v≈0.38634v\approx0.38634v≈0.38634. The direction p=(0.5300,0.8480)T\mathbf p=(0.5300,0.8480)^Tp=(0.5300,0.8480)T is tangent to the rail, enters the feasible side of the fence, and raises the objective to first order. Its negative crosses the fence. Thus there is feasible motion, but no nonzero critical direction at this intersection. “No allowable directions” in a curvature discussion refers to the critical cone, not all feasible motion.

For the displayed rounded vectors, the equality tangent space is one-dimensional: every tangent vector is p=t(0.5300,0.8480)T\mathbf p=t(0.5300,0.8480)^Tp=t(0.5300,0.8480)T, t∈Rt\in\mathbb Rt∈R. The exact dot products of these supplied decimals are

The linearized inequality therefore requires t≥0t\ge0t≥0, and zero first-order objective change then requires t=0t=0t=0. This establishes the whole critical cone {0}\{0\}{0} rather than testing only one direction. These conclusions describe the prepared drawing; the derivatives of the later log-model are calculated independently.

Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) necessary conditions¶

Use row-oriented Jacobians Jg∈Rr×n\mathbf J_g\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}Jg​∈Rr×n and Jh∈Rm×n\mathbf J_h\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}Jh​∈Rm×n and

L(x,u,v)=f(x)+g(x)Tu+h(x)Tv.\mathcal L(\mathbf x,\mathbf u,\mathbf v)=f(\mathbf x)+ \mathbf g(\mathbf x)^T\mathbf u+\mathbf h(\mathbf x)^T\mathbf v.L(x,u,v)=f(x)+g(x)Tu+h(x)Tv.
(4)

Assume C1C^1C1 data on an open neighborhood of a feasible local minimum. If LICQ holds there, the following necessary multiplier conditions apply:

∇xL=∇f+JgTu+JhTv=0,g≤0,h=0,u≥0,giui=0for every i.\begin{aligned} \nabla_{\mathbf x}\mathcal L&=\nabla f+\mathbf J_g^T\mathbf u+\mathbf J_h^T\mathbf v=0,\\ \mathbf g&\le0,\qquad\mathbf h=0,\qquad\mathbf u\ge0,\\ g_i u_i&=0\quad\text{for every }i. \end{aligned}∇x​Lggi​ui​​=∇f+JgT​u+JhT​v=0,≤0,h=0,u≥0,=0for every i.​
(5)

Evaluate these at the candidate. Equality multipliers have no sign restriction. Dot-product complementarity gTu=0\mathbf g^T\mathbf u=0gTu=0 is equivalent to the component conditions because every summand is nonpositive. The prepared product-vector diagram compares nonpositive terms with a zero vector. If any term is strictly negative, no other nonpositive term can cancel it to make the sum zero.

Inactive constraints have gi<0g_i<0gi​<0, hence ui=0u_i=0ui​=0. Active constraints have gi=0g_i=0gi​=0 and may have a positive multiplier (strongly active) or zero multiplier (weakly active).

Regularity is a hypothesis. LICQ means the equality gradients and active inequality gradients are linearly independent. It is one sufficient qualification for the first-order necessary conditions. For min⁡x\min xminx subject to x2=0x^2=0x2=0, the only feasible point is a strict global minimum, but 1+v(2x)=01+v(2x)=01+v(2x)=0 cannot hold at zero for any multiplier. The constraint gradient is zero. See Constraint Qualifications for the full theory.

Feasible and critical directions differ. The linearized feasible cone imposes Jhp=0\mathbf J_h\mathbf p=0Jh​p=0 and ∇giTp≤0\nabla g_i^T\mathbf p\le0∇giT​p≤0 on active inequalities. At a KKT point,

∇fTp=−∑iui∇giTp≥0.\nabla f^T\mathbf p=-\sum_iu_i\nabla g_i^T\mathbf p\ge0.∇fTp=−i∑​ui​∇giT​p≥0.
(6)

Critical directions have zero first-order objective change. Since every term on the right is nonnegative, they are exactly the directions satisfying

Jhp=0,∇giTp=0gi=0, ui>0,∇giTp≤0gi=0, ui=0.\begin{aligned} \mathbf J_h\mathbf p&=0,\\ \nabla g_i^T\mathbf p&=0&&g_i=0,\ u_i>0,\\ \nabla g_i^T\mathbf p&\le0&&g_i=0,\ u_i=0. \end{aligned}Jh​p∇giT​p∇giT​p​=0,=0≤0​​gi​=0, ui​>0,gi​=0, ui​=0.​
(7)

A positive multiplier does not prevent every feasible inward move; it makes that move raise the objective to first order. Such a direction is not critical. These are first-order tangent tests; nonlinear constraints need not remain feasible along an entire straight step.

For g=−x2g=-x_2g=−x2​ at the origin, f=x12+x2f=x_1^2+x_2f=x12​+x2​ has u=1u=1u=1 and critical directions p2=0p_2=0p2​=0. Its feasible directions with p2>0p_2>0p2​>0 already raise the objective. For f=x12+x22f=x_1^2+x_2^2f=x12​+x22​, u=0u=0u=0 and every linearized feasible direction p2≥0p_2\ge0p2​≥0 is critical.

With C2C^2C2 data on an open neighborhood and LICQ at a local minimum, second-order necessity gives

pT∇xx2L p≥0for every critical direction p.\mathbf p^T\nabla_{\mathbf{xx}}^2\mathcal L\,\mathbf p\ge0 \quad\text{for every critical direction }\mathbf p.pT∇xx2​Lp≥0for every critical direction p.
(8)

Together with KKT, strict positivity on every nonzero critical direction is sufficient for a strict local minimum. The two examples satisfy it on their respective cones. If the critical cone is {0}\{0\}{0}, the strict condition is vacuous; feasible noncritical directions are controlled by their positive first-order change. Full theorem/proof material has its existing home in Second-Order Conditions.

Sources: Biegler (2010), Eqs. (4.9)–(4.12), pp.70–71; Theorems 4.14–4.15, pp.77–78; critical cone in §4.3.3, p.79; Theorems 4.17–4.18, pp.80–81. The constraint-qualification counterexample follows directly from its singleton feasible set and zero constraint derivative.

Define Function for Visualization¶

## Objective function
def f(x):
    """Evaluate the two-variable objective function."""
    return (
        x[0] ** 2
        - 4 * x[0]
        + 1.5 * x[1] ** 2
        - 7 * x[1]
        + x[0] * x[1]
        + 9
        - np.log(x[0])
        - np.log(x[1])
    )


## Gradient of objective f(x)
def df(x):
    """Evaluate the objective gradient."""
    return np.array((2 * x[0] - 4 + x[1] - 1 / x[0], 3 * x[1] - 7 + x[0] - 1 / x[1]))


## Gradient of inequality constraint g(x)
def dg(x):
    """Evaluate the inequality-constraint gradient."""
    return np.array((-x[1], -x[0]))


## Gradient of equality constraint h(x)
def dh(x):
    """Evaluate the equality-constraint gradient."""
    return np.array([2, -1])

Define function to solve optimization problem with Pyomo¶

def solve_opt(consider_g, consider_h):
    """Solve the example with the selected constraints enabled."""

    ## Create concrete Pyomo model
    m = pyo.ConcreteModel()

    ## Declare variables with initial values
    m.x1 = pyo.Var(bounds=(0, 100), initialize=10)
    m.x2 = pyo.Var(bounds=(0, 100), initialize=10)

    ## Declare objective
    m.OBJ = pyo.Objective(
        expr=m.x1**2
        - 4 * m.x1
        + 1.5 * m.x2**2
        - 7 * m.x2
        + m.x1 * m.x2
        + 9
        - pyo.log(m.x1)
        - pyo.log(m.x2),
        sense=pyo.minimize,
    )

    if consider_g:
        ## Add inequality constraint
        m.con1 = pyo.Constraint(expr=4 - m.x1 * m.x2 <= 0)

    if consider_h:
        ## Add equality constraint
        m.con2 = pyo.Constraint(expr=2 * m.x1 - m.x2 == 0)

    ## Specify IPOPT as solver
    solver = pyo.SolverFactory("ipopt")

    ## Solve the model
    results = solver.solve(m, tee=True)
    assert pyo.check_optimal_termination(results), (
        f"Solve failed: status={results.solver.status}, "
        f"termination={results.solver.termination_condition}"
    )

    ## Return the solution
    return [pyo.value(m.x1), pyo.value(m.x2)]

This particular example also admits a global certificate. On its positive log domain, 4−x1x2≤04-x_1x_2\le04−x1​x2​≤0 is equivalent to 4/x1−x2≤04/x_1-x_2\le04/x1​−x2​≤0, a convex inequality; its second derivative in x1x_1x1​ is 8/x13>08/x_1^3>08/x13​>0.
